Free & legal games

People are still making brand-new games for the NES, Game Boy, Sega Master System, Game Gear and CHIP-8 — thousands of them, many free. Here's where to find them, how to tell what you're allowed to do with them, and how to get them onto your RetroCYD.

The short legal version

Not legal advice — just the plain facts most people get wrong.

✓ LEGAL

Emulators

An emulator is software that behaves like old hardware. Writing, sharing and using one — like RetroCYD — is legal in most countries.

✗ NOT OK

Downloading commercial ROMs you don't own

Copying commercial games you haven't bought is copyright infringement in most countries — even if the game is decades old or no longer sold. “Abandonware” isn't a legal category.

✓ WHAT RETROCYD IS FOR

Homebrew, public domain, free licences, your own backups

New games released by their authors for free, public-domain programs, openly licensed projects — and backups of cartridges you own, where your local law allows it.

RetroCYD ships with no games, and this site only links to sources where authors chose to share their work. We will never link to ROM-piracy sites — please don't ask.

Checklist

How to add games

  • The game is free to download (homebrew, public domain, open licence) — or a backup of a cartridge you own.
  • Unzipping is optional. A .zip containing a single ROM works as-is.
  • Drop it into the right folder: /roms/nes, /roms/gb, /roms/gbc or /roms/chip8.
  • File names are shown as titles — rename astro_drift_v1.2_final.gb to Astro Drift.gb.
  • Huge library? Use subfolders. The first visit to a big folder builds an index (a few seconds for ~10,000 files), then it's instant.
  • Keep each ROM ≤ 2 MB (the size of the flash ROM cache).
  • CHIP-8: only CHIP-8 and SUPER-CHIP programs — not XO-CHIP.
  • Eject the card safely. Missing something? Settings → Rescan library.

Reading licences in 30 seconds

“Free to download” doesn't always mean “free to re-upload”. Here's what the common labels let you do.

LabelPlay it?Share the file?
Public domain / CC0YesYes — no conditions at all.
Open source (MIT, zlib, GPL, CC-BY…)YesYes, under the licence's conditions — usually keep the credits and licence text.
FreewareYesOften restricted — link to the author's page instead of re-uploading.
Pay what you wantYesSame as freeware. $0 is allowed; paying something is how authors keep making games.
Commercial homebrewAfter buying itNo. If it's for sale, buy it — that's how the scene survives.

Own the cartridge?

Back up your own cartridges

Cartridge dumpers plug into your computer over USB and copy a cartridge's ROM (and often its save) to a file.

GBxCart RW

A reader/writer for Game Boy, Game Boy Color and Game Boy Advance cartridges, sold through insideGadgets and used with the FlashGBX software. Backs up ROMs and save data.

gbxcart.com

INL Retro Programmer-Dumper

An open-source cartridge dumper/programmer from InfiniteNESLives that reads NES / Famicom cartridges (among others). Similar dumpers exist from other makers.

INL-retro-progdump on GitLab

Personal backups: check your local law

Whether you may copy a cartridge you own varies by country — some allow private backups, some don't, and some only under conditions. Check what applies where you live. Either way: keep your dumps to yourself and never share them.
