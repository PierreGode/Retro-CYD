RETROCYD

Turn a Cheap Yellow Display into a pocket retro console.

Free firmware for the 4.0″ ESP32 touch board that plays NES, Game Boy, Game Boy Color and CHIP-8 homebrew from a microSD card. Flash it straight from your browser — no toolchain, no command line.

What you get

A real little console, not a tech demo

Everything below is in the firmware today. Things that aren't done yet live on the roadmap, not here.

Four systems

NES (Famicom), Game Boy, Game Boy Color and CHIP-8 / SUPER-CHIP — all in one firmware.

Launcher for huge libraries

System tiles, a recently-played list, an A–Z quick-jump bar and touch-drag scrolling. Big folders get a sorted index on the SD card the first time (a few seconds for ~10,000 files), then open instantly. Subfolders welcome.

Flash ROM cache

Games are copied from SD into a 2 MB flash partition and run from there. A new game takes a couple of seconds (with a progress bar); relaunching the same one is instant. ZIPs with a single ROM work too.

Saves that stick

Battery saves (.sav) are written to the SD card when you open the menu, quit, and periodically while playing. Plus 4 save-state slots per game.

Menu & settings

In game: resume, save/load state, reset, screen scale, volume, quit — open it with BOOT, the on-screen MENU button, or HOME on a controller. Settings: backlight, fast display, FPS counter, touch calibration, Game Boy palette and more.

Touch or real buttons

An on-screen gamepad for quick play, or plug in a Wii Classic Controller (Pro) or a NES / SNES Classic Mini controller via the I2C header. Hot-plug, auto-detected.

Actual sound

Audio through the board's DAC and on-board amplifier to the 1.25 mm speaker connector. Add a small 8 Ω or 4 Ω speaker and you're done.

Free games over Wi-Fi

Pick your Wi-Fi in Settings and RetroCYD downloads about 1,000 free homebrew games from Homebrew Hub and the CHIP-8 Archive — automatically at startup, or when you tap “Download now”. Each file is checked before it's kept. Firmware updates install over Wi-Fi too.

Web installer

Flash it from your browser

Uses ESP Web Tools and the Web Serial API. Works in desktop Chrome, Edge and Opera. It only ever writes the flash chip — it never burns eFuses.

  1. Plug the board in

    Use a USB-C data cable. Many cables in drawers are charge-only and the board simply won't show up.

  2. Pick your board

    RetroCYD is built for the 4.0″ ESP32-32E (E32R40T with touch, E32N40T without). Other CYD variants use different displays and pins.

  3. Connect & choose the port

    Click the button and pick the port called something like USB-SERIAL CH340 or USB Serial. Nothing listed? You may need the CH340 driver.

  4. Install

    On a first install tick Erase device — it wipes old firmware and settings from the flash chip only. Your SD card is never touched.

  5. Done — it reboots

    Wait for the progress bar to finish (usually a minute or two). The board restarts into RetroCYD. Now set up the SD card.

“Failed to connect”? Hold BOOT while you click Connect, release once it starts. More fixes in Troubleshooting.

After flashing

From flashed to playing

Four steps. The full details are in the guide.

  1. Format a microSD

    FAT32. Cards up to 32 GB work out of the box; bigger cards must be reformatted to FAT32.

  2. Boot once with it

    RetroCYD creates /roms/nes, /roms/gb, /roms/gbc, /roms/chip8 and /saves for you.

  3. Add free games

    Drop homebrew ROMs (or ZIPs) into the right folder. Not sure where to get them legally? We made a list.

  4. Play

    Tap a system tile, pick a game. BOOT, on-screen MENU or HOME opens the in-game menu.

Not possible on this hardware

The board has one ESP32 (dual-core Xtensa LX6, 240 MHz) with 520 KB of internal RAM, no PSRAM, 4 MB of flash, and a display that is fed pixel-by-pixel over SPI. That's plenty for 8-bit machines and nowhere near enough for these:

  • SNESThree processors to emulate at once (65816 CPU, picture processor, SPC700 sound) — far too much work for full speed — and many ROMs are bigger than the 2 MB cache.
  • GBAA 32-bit ARM with ~384 KB of RAM/VRAM to emulate, and ROMs up to 32 MB — far past the 2 MB cache and the RAM budget.
  • Genesis / Mega DriveA 68000 plus a Z80 and a demanding video chip is too much for one ESP32 without PSRAM, and the larger ROMs (up to 4 MB) exceed the cache.
  • N64 · PS13D consoles with megabytes of RAM and CD- or cartridge-sized data. Orders of magnitude beyond a microcontroller.

Honesty box

“Is this actually possible?”

Yes — with realistic expectations. Here's how it feels in practice.

NES — game speedfull 60 Hz

Emulation runs at full speed; the display uses automatic frame-skip.

NES / Game Boy — displayed frames~30–60 fps

Depends on screen scale and the “fast display” setting.

Game Boy / Color — game speedfull speed
CHIP-8 / SUPER-CHIPtrivially fast
  • Touch: the E32R40T panel is resistive and single-touch — fine for menus, puzzle and turn-based games. For platformers and shooters, plug in a wired controller.
  • ROM size: max 2 MB. Typical homebrew is far smaller; the few 4 MB and 8 MB Game Boy Color titles won't fit.
  • Game switching: a couple of seconds to copy a new ROM into flash; relaunching the same game is instant.

Why frame-skip? Back-of-the-envelope SPI math

Every pixel travels to the ST7796S as 16 bits over SPI. Raw transfer ceilings (before any emulation work):

PicturePixels40 MHz80 MHz*
Game Boy 160×14423,040≈108 fps≈217 fps
Game Boy ×2 → 320×28892,160≈27 fps≈54 fps
NES 256×24061,440≈41 fps≈81 fps
Full screen 480×320153,600≈16 fps≈33 fps

* “Fast display” in Settings overclocks the display SPI from 40 to 80 MHz. Most panels handle it; if you see glitches, switch it off. The emulated game always runs at its real speed — only the number of frames shown changes.

Hard rule

We never burn fuses.

The ESP32 has one-time-programmable eFuses for things like secure boot and flash encryption. Burning them is permanent. RetroCYD never goes near them — not the web flasher, not the CI build, not the firmware.

Safe “Erase device”

Wipes the 4 MB SPI flash chip: old firmware, RetroCYD settings and the ROM cache. Your games and saves on the SD card are untouched. 100% reversible — just flash again.

Never Burning eFuses

Permanent changes inside the ESP32 chip itself. Secure boot and flash encryption are disabled in our build and nothing is ever run through espefuse.

Flashing manually? Only ever use esptool.py --chip esp32 write_flash 0x0 retrocyd-cyd40-merged.bin. Full details, recovery steps and the list of commands to avoid are in the guide.
